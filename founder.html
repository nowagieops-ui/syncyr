Meet the team

Dr Bindunie Sirimanne Rowland is a British entrepreneur, AI scientist and inventor whose work connects medicine, human behaviour, technology and institutional trust. A physician by training, she brings a polymath approach to research and company leadership, drawing on a career across military medicine, business, international relations, wellness and the performing arts.

At CEYMIRION and SYNCYR, she leads the research, invention and architectural development behind a question that brings these interests together: how can an artificial intelligence system preserve coherent behaviour, identity continuity and accountable decision-making as its context, underlying model and operating environment change?

Her path to that question has crossed hospital wards, boardrooms, international stages and community projects. Each has added to her understanding of people and of the responsibility that comes with building something others will depend upon.

01

Artificial intelligence and the building of an enterprise

Her move into AI grew from an enquiry into technology’s influence on human attention, cognition and agency. Sustained work with conversational systems brought the behaviour of intelligence over time into focus: the patterns that recur, the moments when continuity breaks, and the conditions under which it can be restored.

She originated Relational Attractor Yoking (R.A.Y.), a proposed research framework examining how sustained, structured human interaction may influence behavioural stability in fixed-weight generative models. Its questions concern continuity, drift, recovery and the recurring patterns that emerge through extended human–AI interaction.

By September 2026, the programme’s recorded history covered more than 450 days of longitudinal observation, over 750 interaction threads and more than 4,500 hours of founder-led research and operation across multiple model families. This continuing record provides material for studying what happens as relationships with AI extend across changing contexts, models and platforms.

The enquiry took corporate form through CEYMIRION, the intellectual-property and research company, and SYNCYR, its operating and commercialisation arm. As founder, she directs the progression from original research and architectural specifications to engineering, evaluation and institutional engagement. By September 2026, the portfolio comprised nine UK patent applications and two PCT international filings, alongside six R.A.Y. papers deposited on Zenodo as reports, a working paper and a preprint, and two earlier architecture publications.

Supported by £50,000 in external founding capital, she led the development of the research, intellectual-property portfolio and commercial foundations during the first company year, including early paid deployments and documented international institutional relationships.

The engineering team has translated her specifications into working implementations across all seven core SYNCYR layers, connected to a live model API. By September 2026, SEICS, the architecture’s epistemic-integrity layer, had completed more than twenty structured internal test cycles and was the first priority for independent external evaluation. She works with engineers and scientific advisers to connect this progress with reproducible testing, academic collaboration and institutional deployment.

In November 2025, she presented RAY, the SYNCYR prototype, in a live multilingual demonstration at a Creative Women Platform event at Princess Nourah bint Abdulrahman University in Riyadh, before an audience of approximately one hundred.

02

Medicine and the beginnings of leadership

Born in Sri Lanka into an artistic and entrepreneurial family, she encountered creative expression and commercial responsibility early in life. Seven years of education in Russia broadened that perspective across another language and culture. She completed her Doctor of Medicine at Russian State Medical University in Moscow in 1999, then returned to Sri Lanka to begin work as a Medical Officer at Matale Base Hospital.

Her early board-level experience came through her family’s business, an enterprise employing approximately 3,000 people. Between 1999 and 2009, she worked within its executive leadership, with responsibilities spanning strategic and commercial decisions, business development and workforce welfare. She helped develop medical welfare policy, worked across human resources and represented the company in negotiations with overseas buyers. It was an education in leadership at organisational scale, where the wellbeing of employees and the direction of the business had to be considered together.

From 1999 to 2002, during Sri Lanka’s civil war, she also served as a Captain and Medical Officer in the Sri Lanka Army Medical Force (Volunteer), including as Deputy In Charge of Diyatalawa Military Base Hospital. Clinical care sat alongside hospital administration, public relations and educational responsibilities. Her artistic life continued even within military service, through performances with the military band at national events.

In 2002, she moved into senior hospital management at Apollo Hospitals, leading patient facilities, front-office operations and international patient services. Her remit combined operational decision-making with the development of guest relations, registration and patient feedback processes, alongside coordination of international referrals to Apollo Hospitals in India. This brought her medical understanding into the organisation of care across departments, institutions and national borders.

03

Enterprise and the understanding of wellbeing

Her move to the United Kingdom opened a new chapter. Following work in healthcare support and administration, she established Venuz Transformz in 2011 and began building a practice in medical laser and light therapies. Its early growth led to a business partnership at Bedford Skin Clinic, where her work combined clinical practice with commercial development.

At Bedford, she helped grow the active laser clientele from approximately one hundred to more than seven hundred. She subsequently established and led a boutique cosmetic practice in Buckinghamshire, followed by further ventures in cosmetic medicine and wellness. Her responsibilities encompassed the development of services, business direction and relationships with private clients, including public figures and international patients.

Her work also continued across borders. In Moscow, she combined a visiting clinical consultancy with a business partnership at a cosmetic research and training centre, introducing her Venuz concept to its offering.

In 2021, she founded WellBeology, leading its strategy, project development and international partnerships. Working with collaborators across the UK, USA, Russia, Japan, Korea, India and the UAE, she developed wellness concepts and programmes concerned with biological age, premature ageing and wellbeing.

These years deepened her interest in the relationship between physiology, psychology and human adaptation. Mental health education, mindfulness and the experience of self-image became continuing concerns. They also helped shape the questions she would later bring to technology: how people respond to change, what supports their agency, and how trust is formed through repeated experience.

04

An artistic life

Performance entered her life at the age of four and has accompanied every chapter since. As a singer, dancer, poet and television performer, she has worked internationally in Sri Lanka and Russia, including appearances on Russian Channel 1.

She sings in nine languages: Sinhala, Tamil, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, English, Russian, Spanish and Arabic.

Her music research project, The Fifth Element, brings her concept of Music Without Borders into practice through multilingual reinterpretations of familiar classics. Songs flow between languages within a single performance, retaining their original meaning as she explores how melody and expression connect listeners across cultures. The project is guided by her belief that music, like love, can create a meeting place beyond words, where cultural barriers soften and shared feeling finds its voice.

This artistic experience gives her polymath approach its attention to rhythm, expression and the subtleties of communication. Across a song, a conversation or a public address, meaning depends on how it reaches another person. Her work in the arts has also connected with charitable programmes that offer people with disabilities opportunities for creative expression.

05

Boards and international public engagement

Her experience of company leadership, medicine and creative practice also informs her board and advisory work. She serves on the Creative Women Platform’s Advisory Board and as an Ambassador, and holds an advisory board appointment with the Eco-Friendly Web Alliance (EFWA).

Her contributions as a speaker and judge span women’s leadership, medicine, innovation and creative enterprise. Alongside Sinhala, her mother tongue, she uses English, Russian, Spanish and Hindi in her international work. International appearances have included Creative Women Platform events in London and Riyadh, AIM Congress in Abu Dhabi, AMWC events in London and Moscow, and FACE in London. These engagements bring her experience as a founder, executive and practitioner into conversation with people working across different industries and cultures.

06

Philanthropy and community

Following the 2004 Sri Lankan tsunami, while on maternity leave, she took responsibility as Officer in Charge of a relief camp accommodating approximately 4,000 people. Between 2006 and 2008, she volunteered with the Sunera Foundation, supporting performing arts programmes for people with disabilities.

Since 2016, she has served as Trustee and Secretary of S.O.U.P., Sincere Offerings for Unappreciated Persons, a UK charity supporting people who are neglected or overlooked. She also established a scholarship programme in Sri Lanka that funds the educational costs of twenty students from low-income backgrounds each year.

Her commitment to education has included service as a Parent Governor at Holywell Primary School in Watford and on the parents’ association committee at Chesham Grammar School. Across relief work, disability arts, scholarships and school governance, her contribution has taken different forms while remaining close to the needs of people and their communities.

07

Education and continuing enquiry

Her school years offered an early glimpse of the breadth that would shape her life: she served as a prefect and received repeated awards for best student. Alongside her studies and leadership responsibilities, she won national-level competitions in music and dance, and district-level competitions in athletics.

Alongside her medical degree, she holds a Postgraduate Diploma in International Relations from the Bandaranaike Centre for International Studies. She has completed a Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy certificate course at the Oxford Mindfulness Centre and is currently studying towards an MSc in Psychology and Neuroscience of Mental Health at King’s College London.

She is a Fellow of the Royal Society of Medicine and of the Royal Society for Public Health. Her continuing education reflects the same curiosity that connects the different parts of her working life: how people think, adapt, communicate and find their place in changing environments.

08

The experience behind the vision

By the time she founded CEYMIRION and SYNCYR, Dr Rowland had spent more than two decades holding responsibility for people, services and organisations. Directorships, hospital management, military service and the building of her own businesses had given her experience of turning ideas into operations, working across cultures and making decisions with consequences beyond herself.

Her work in AI carries that experience forward. Scientific enquiry gives it a method; enterprise gives it a means of becoming useful; the arts and community work keep its human questions in view. At CEYMIRION and SYNCYR, these strands meet in a continuing ambition: to build intelligence that preserves human agency and earns trust over time.
